UI/UX Group Project Submission

UI/UX Architecture Decision: "Pages vs. Scroll" for an Equity Research Blog

Comprehensive evaluation addressing project requirement: "We're tasked to submit minimum 4 blogs, so decide if it's better if it's pages or scroll."

Our Group Decision: The Hybrid "Card Feed Hub + Dedicated Article Pages"

For an institutional equity research blog covering a high-complexity stock like Broadcom ($AVGO), Dedicated Pages (Page-based routing) are decisively superior to a single infinite scroll. Below is the multi-dimensional UX justification developed for our university submission.

1. Comparative Analysis: Pages vs. Continuous Scroll

Evaluation Dimension Dedicated Pages (Our Choice) Continuous Long Scroll
Cognitive Load & Reading Fatigue Low: Reader focuses on one thesis at a time (e.g. Valuation vs Bear Case). High: 4,000+ words of dense financial data creates severe "scroll fatigue."
Academic Grading & Citation Direct URL hash (#post-1, #post-2) allows professors to grade specific memos. Difficult to cite; reader must guess scroll percentage.
Media & Performance (Videos/Charts) Media isolated per page; rapid rendering; smooth progress tracking. Heavier initial DOM weight; stacked videos lag on mobile viewports.
Information Architecture & Scent Home dashboard offers instant scanning of Target Prices, Ratings, and Dates. Buried content; user must scroll through Post 1 to discover Post 4.
Industry Benchmark Alignment Standard for Bloomberg Intelligence, Morningstar, Morgan Stanley Research, Substack. Common only for casual social feeds (Twitter/Instagram), not equity research.

2. The Five Core UX Principles Behind Our Decision

A. Scent of Information & Executive Skimmability

Institutional investors, portfolio managers, and university professors do not read financial research linearly from top to bottom. They scan first: What is the rating? What is the 12-month target price? What are the key catalysts? Our Home Dashboard presents all 4 memos as discrete cards with financial pills (Rating, Target, Upside, Read Time). The user chooses when to invest their attention.

B. Isolation of Deep Reading Focus

When reading a DCF valuation model with mathematical tables and margin trajectories, visual clutter is toxic to comprehension. Moving to a dedicated page clears out external distractions, activates the reading progress bar, and unlocks reading comfort tools (text scaling from A- to A+).

C. Shareability and Deep Linking

Every blog post has an individual slug and anchor hash (e.g. #post-1 for Initiation, #post-5 for the Bear Case). If our group wants to present our Bear Case in a university seminar, we can link directly to that specific slide or URL without forcing the audience to scroll past three unrelated articles.

D. Video & Image Asset Performance

Financial blogs frequently feature executive interviews (Hock Tan on CNBC) and heavy architectural diagrams (Tomahawk 5 spine-leaf topology). Stacking four full-length video players and multiple infographics onto a single continuous webpage slows down initial paint time and consumes significant bandwidth on mobile devices.

E. The Live Toggle Demonstration

To prove our thesis to our university evaluator, we built a live interactive toggle in the header: clicking "Scroll" renders all 4 articles sequentially, while clicking "Pages" isolates them. Testing both modes side-by-side demonstrates that Pages provide vastly superior readability and mental retention.
